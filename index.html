About Me

Research across consumers, producers, and agricultural systems

I am a PhD student in Agricultural, Food, and Resource Economics at Michigan State University. My research uses applied microeconomics, discrete choice experiments, experimental measures of risk preferences, and production economics to understand decision-making by consumers and agricultural producers.

My earlier research has a strong consumer and crop-commodity component, including willingness to pay and preference heterogeneity for chile pepper attributes, certification, branding, and sustainability information. My current work emphasizes producer behavior, conservation-contract design, risk, grazing management, and U.S. cow-calf production.

Methods & Research Tools

Experimental design, survey development & applied econometrics

DESIGN

Ngene Experimental Design

Experience developing D-efficient and Bayesian-efficient discrete choice experiments, including attributes and levels, priors, blocking, and design implementation.

SURVEY

Qualtrics Survey Development

Experience developing complex Qualtrics surveys with screening, randomization, information treatments, repeated choice tasks, respondent-flow logic, pretesting, incentives, and quality controls.

CHOICE

Choice & Behavioral Methods

Discrete choice experiments, willingness-to-pay analysis, mixed/random-parameter logit, preference heterogeneity, and Holt–Laury risk elicitation.

ECON

Production & Applied Econometrics

Production economics, stochastic frontier analysis, technical efficiency, agricultural profitability, and empirical analysis of crop, livestock, and environmental questions.

Research Agenda

Looking ahead
