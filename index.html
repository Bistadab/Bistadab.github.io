About

Research at the intersection of producer behavior and agricultural systems

I am a PhD student in Agricultural, Food, and Resource Economics at Michigan State University. My research uses applied microeconomics, choice experiments, experimental measures of risk preferences, and production economics to understand how agricultural producers respond to economic incentives, risk, and conservation requirements.

Before MSU, I completed an M.S. in Agricultural Economics with a minor in Statistics at New Mexico State University and a B.S. in Agriculture at Agriculture and Forestry University in Nepal. My earlier research includes consumer valuation, chile production economics, crop profitability, and agricultural sustainability.

Research Areas

Current research

01

Producer Risk Preferences

Measuring agricultural producers’ risk preferences and examining how elicitation methods—including Holt–Laury tasks and discrete choice experiments—shape inference about risk aversion.

02

Conservation Contract Design

Studying ranchers’ willingness to participate in voluntary grazing-conservation programs and the compensation associated with increasingly demanding pasture-rest requirements.

03

Production Efficiency & Risk

Investigating technical inefficiency in U.S. cow-calf operations and how experimentally elicited producer risk preferences relate to production performance.

04

Grazing & Ecosystem Services

Economic analysis of grazing management, producer profitability, soil carbon, and ecosystem services within interdisciplinary grazingland research.

Research Agenda

Looking ahead

My research agenda is centered on understanding agricultural producer behavior and designing economically informed approaches to production, risk management, and conservation. I am particularly interested in combining experimental and observational data to study producer decision-making, agricultural efficiency, and the economic–environmental tradeoffs of land and livestock management.
